# Evidence policy

## Purpose

This policy governs evidence used by the Actual AI Adoption engine and evidence supporting capability/practicality assessments. Its main aim is to prevent unsupported estimates and false precision while preserving provenance.

## Adoption evidence grades

Evidence grade is assigned for a particular intended estimate, not to a publisher in the abstract.

| Grade | Standard | Typical fit |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Recent, representative official data directly measuring the target country and industry with a clear method and denominator | National statistical agency survey with a usable industry cross-tab |
| **B** | Strong industry, academic, or independent research with credible sampling and a close match to the intended population | Transparent, well-designed sector survey or peer-reviewed study |
| **C** | Credible but broader, indirect, or inherited evidence | Parent-sector data applied to a child industry with disclosed inheritance |
| **D** | Weak proxy, limited sample, unclear representativeness, or materially mismatched definition | Vendor/customer survey used cautiously as contextual evidence |
| **Insufficient** | No defensible quantitative estimate for the intended scope | Anecdotes, marketing claims, or no relevant evidence |

Recency is judged against the pace of change and must not rescue a weak design. Official status alone does not guarantee grade A; the observation must directly fit the geography, industry, population, and construct.

## Required observation metadata

Every adoption observation records, where available:

- source identifier and source URL;
- publisher and source title;
- publication date and access date;
- observation period or reference date;
- geography and geographic level;
- source industry/category label and mapped internal industry;
- sample size and relevant subgroup sample size;
- the source's definition of AI;
- measured concept and question wording;
- denominator/population base;
- methodology and weighting notes;
- value representation and unit;
- evidence grade and rationale;
- whether evidence is direct or inherited;
- whether the value is observed or modeled;
- inheritance origin and rationale, when inherited;
- model method and inputs, when modeled;
- confidence level/range and its basis, when applicable; and
- extraction notes, locator, and reviewer status.

Unavailable metadata is marked unknown rather than guessed.

## Value and lineage states

An observation's numeric representation and provenance are separate:

- **Point:** a reported value, optionally with a defensible confidence interval.
- **Range:** lower and upper bounds when the source or synthesis supports only an interval.
- **Missing:** no defensible quantitative value; includes a reason and may reference sources reviewed.
- **Observed:** directly reported by the cited source for its stated population.
- **Inherited:** an observed value borrowed from a broader or adjacent geography/industry/size group. The origin and inheritance logic are mandatory.
- **Modeled:** a future estimate generated by a declared method rather than measured directly. Model version, input observations, validation, and uncertainty are mandatory.

`Missing` is a value state. `Observed`, `inherited`, and `modeled` describe provenance. A directly observed point and an inherited point therefore share a numeric form but not an evidence lineage.

## Direct and inherited evidence

Direct evidence matches the target estimate on the material geography, industry, population, firm-size definition, measured construct, and period. Inherited evidence relaxes at least one of those dimensions. An inherited observation must identify:

1. the parent or proxy observation;
2. dimensions that do and do not match;
3. why inheritance is reasonable;
4. any adjustment applied; and
5. the resulting downgrade in evidence grade or confidence.

Inheritance must never be hidden behind a target-industry label.

## Modeled estimates

Modeled estimates are not part of the initial seed data. If introduced later, they require a separately approved modeling methodology, model version, input lineage, validation results, assumptions, and uncertainty interval. Interfaces reserve this state so that it cannot be confused with observation.

Publication and APIs must expose provenance alongside the value. A modeled result must be labeled `modeled`; it cannot be relabeled `observed` after review or aggregation.

## Confidence

Confidence includes a qualitative level (`high`, `medium`, `low`, or `unknown`), rationale, and optional numeric interval. A numeric interval must state its method and level (for example, a source-reported 95% confidence interval). When no defensible interval exists, the system should use qualitative confidence or a broad evidence-supported range instead of manufacturing bounds.

Evidence grade and confidence should generally weaken when evidence is old, the subgroup is small, source definitions are ambiguous, mappings are approximate, or evidence is inherited/modelled. Any exception requires a rationale.

## Normalization

Raw observations are immutable. Normalization creates a derived observation or score that references:

- every input observation;
- a named and versioned transformation;
- unit and scale conversions;
- category crosswalks;
- assumptions; and
- uncertainty propagation, if applicable.

Values measuring unlike constructs—such as any AI use, generative-AI use, employee usage, firm adoption, expenditure, or task coverage—must not be averaged merely because each is expressed as a percentage.

For Gap eligibility, a percentage additionally needs a declared mapping to the same business function, task/use case, Standard/Integrated/Advanced depth, and geography as Possible. It must measure task utilization or task coverage and carry the identical approved normalized-scale ID. Broad country/sector prevalence and tool-use frequency retain a null task/depth mapping and are never reclassified as direct task-level utilization.

Proxy evidence remains identifiable in the comparison mapping and lowers a synthesized result's confidence by one qualitative level. Modeled evidence remains labeled modeled and lowers confidence by one additional level. Neither proxy nor modeled evidence contributes to the v0.1 numeric-coverage threshold.

## Avoiding false precision

- Preserve source precision internally but publish only justified precision.
- Prefer ranges when mappings or inheritance introduce meaningful uncertainty.
- Do not infer zero from non-response, absence of evidence, or “not reported.”
- Do not convert qualitative statements to numbers without an approved modeling method.
- Do not pool observations without accounting for population, construct, time, and sampling differences.
- Show the evidence grade, provenance state, observation period, and confidence with the result.

## Review and audit

Each production observation should record extractor and reviewer identifiers plus timestamps. Material corrections create new versions or superseding records; they do not erase the original. Evidence used in a score remains addressable for as long as that score is published.
